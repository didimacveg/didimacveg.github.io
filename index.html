gravity-lab

Shipped
C++17 · CMake · raylib · JavaScript · GitHub Actions

An N-body simulator of the Solar System, validated against astronomical measurement rather than eyeballed. Real J2000 initial conditions, four integrators, and a black-hole module that uses the Schwarzschild innermost stable circular orbit as its capture criterion.

With a first post-Newtonian correction enabled it reproduces the anomalous precession of Mercury's perihelion — the discrepancy Le Verrier measured in 1859 and general relativity explained in 1915. The control case, with no perturbations and no relativity, returns exactly zero: without that, the rest would be numerical error rather than physics.

GetSympto+

Finished · paused
Next.js · Supabase · Claude Haiku · Stripe · Vercel

Symptom orientation platform. Users describe what they feel and pick the affected area on an interactive body map; the app returns possible causes, an urgency level and next steps — in four languages, anonymous by default.

Reached a functional MVP and shipped to production. Paused: the payment providers I'd built around rejected the app for being health-adjacent, which forced a migration to Stripe that I haven't finished. Technical feasibility doesn't guarantee business feasibility — that's the most useful thing this project taught me.

About

I'm interested in the part of AI that touches the physical world: robotics, control, perception. Software that only moves data around stops being interesting to me fairly quickly.

Most of what I know is self-taught, which shows in both directions — I can build a distributed system with agents and a sandbox, and I still have gaps a formal education would have closed. That's part of why I want to study this somewhere it's taken seriously.

I use AI heavily as a development tool and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise. The architecture, the trade-offs and the decisions about what to build are mine, and I can defend every one of them.
